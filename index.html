Kelly Criterion Project

May 2024 – July 2024
Website

A Villanova Mathematics professor reached out after a recommendation from Dr. Alexander Diaz-Lopez, with whom I'd completed prior Mathematics research, needing a betting-game website to demonstrate the Kelly Criterion. Over email, he specified the requirements:

  • A player starts with $100 in capital.
  • Each round, they choose a whole-percentage stake (0–100%) of their current capital to bet on heads for the next coin toss.
  • The coin has an adjustable probability of landing heads (default p = 0.6).
    • A win adds the bet amount to the player's capital.
    • A loss subtracts it.
  • Play continues for an adjustable maximum of N = 100 tosses.
  • Each round: choose a stake, the coin is "tossed" via random number generation, and the outcome updates the balance.
  • Reaching $1,000 or more at any point ends the game with a $1,000 payout.
  • Betting 100% and losing ends the game immediately with a $0 payout.
  • Otherwise, the player keeps whatever is in their account after 100 rounds.

I iterated on the build over the summer, sending versions to the professor by email and incorporating his feedback into each revision. Game results were submitted to a Google Form and auto-formatted into a connected Google Sheet, giving him direct access to analyze player data. The professor has since stopped actively using the site, though it still logs submissions to the sheet.

Research

The Language of Over-Consumption: Insights from YouTube Haul Transcripts

January 2025 – August 2026
Undergraduate Research Assistant

A continuation of my earlier #sheinhaul and #thrifthaul research with the same Computer Science team under Dr. Sarah Cooney, situated within Sustainable Human-Computer Interaction (SHCI). I built Python programs using the YouTube Transcript API to generate transcripts of #haul videos, and used Beautiful Soup to scrape related sustainability articles from newspaper databases. The team performed lexical analysis comparing #sheinhaul and #thrifthaul transcripts against the sustainability press coverage, and wrote up our findings for potential publication.

#AntiHaul: Creator Motivation and Anti-Consumption

May 2024 – December 2024
VURF Program & Undergraduate Research Assistant Presented Poster

Started through Villanova's Undergraduate Research Fellowship (VURF) over the summer and continued into a formal Undergraduate Research Assistant position for Fall 2024, under Dr. Sarah Cooney. This project examined the #antihaul trend on YouTube — an extension of my earlier #sheinhaul and #thrifthaul work — within the SHCI space. I wrote Python programs against YouTube's Public Data API to build a candidate video dataset, then manually reviewed each entry for relevance given the API's inconsistency. The team coded creator motivations against viewer engagement and found minimal correlation between the two. I designed and presented a poster on this work at Villanova's Undergraduate Research Symposium in November 2024, and again at the VU Women in Tech conference in January 2025.

#Haul: An Analysis of YouTube Discourse Surrounding Over-Consumption and Sustainability in Haul Videos

January 2024 – May 2024
MATCH Program Published Paper

Joined this Computer Science research team under Dr. Sarah Cooney through Villanova's First-Year MATCH Program. The project, categorized under Sustainable Human-Computer Interaction (SHCI), examined the #haul trend on YouTube through the #sheinhaul and #thrifthaul lenses. I built Python programs against YouTube's Public Data API to collect and analyze roughly 1,000 videos across two subsets, one per hashtag, which the team then manually verified given the API's inconsistency. We coded each video, description, and comment section for pro- or anti-consumption themes and analyzed the results with a focus on sustainability across video-sharing platforms, applying data modeling, text analysis, and visualization techniques throughout. The resulting paper was accepted and published at NordiCHI 2024.

Non-Recursive Solution of the Tower of Hanoi

January 2024 – May 2024
Co-Master Program Published Paper Website

Joined a Mathematics research team under Dr. Alexander Diaz-Lopez through Villanova's Co-Master Program to explore the Tower of Hanoi's known most-efficient solution, aiming to derive an equation predicting which peg holds any given disk at any given move. As the only computer scientist on the team, I wrote Python simulations to verify our equation against millions of generated inputs before the team formalized the result through mathematical proof. We presented our work at the EPaDel conference at Albright College and at Villanova's Mathematics Symposium; I also built an informal, mobile-friendly website letting conference attendees enter any move number and see the resulting position of every disk in a 50-disk tower. The paper was published in Mathematics Magazine in July 2025.
